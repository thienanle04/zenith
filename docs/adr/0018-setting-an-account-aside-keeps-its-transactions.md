# Setting an account aside keeps its transactions

An account can be set aside. Its transactions stay, and its balance leaves assets, liabilities, and net worth. A set-aside stock account's share holdings stay in the book. Deleting it completely removes the account and those transactions. A transfer or a trade is one record, so the other account loses that leg and a holding loses that quantity. A holding or an instrument is not set aside: removing it removes its movements immediately.

Leaving the balance inside net worth would hide the row and keep the figure, which is not setting it aside. Removing the transactions on the first step would rewrite the other account before the owner had asked for that.
