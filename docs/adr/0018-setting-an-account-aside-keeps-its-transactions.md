# Setting an account aside keeps its transactions

An account can be set aside. Its transactions stay, and its balance leaves assets, liabilities, and net worth. Setting aside a stock account removes its share holdings, and those holdings take their trades and holding adjustments with them, so the cash those trades moved comes off that account. An instrument with nothing left held is removed. Deleting an account completely removes the account and those transactions. A transfer or a trade is one record, so the other account loses that leg and a holding loses that quantity. A holding or an instrument is not set aside: removing it removes its movements immediately.

Leaving the share holdings in place would keep their value in net worth after the stock account had left it. Leaving the balance inside net worth would hide the row and keep the figure, which is not setting it aside. Removing every transaction on the first step would rewrite the other account before the owner had asked for that.
